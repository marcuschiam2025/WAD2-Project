# Track Finder quiz — draft for team review

**Status:** Content draft for Vignesh, Marcus, and the wider team to review with senior IS students or TAs. It is not a validated psychometric assessment or official academic advice. The team must design, implement, and test the matching and recommendation business logic itself, in line with the assignment brief.

## Purpose and source

Help Year 1–2 Information Systems students explore four tracks without assuming they have taken specialised courses. The first result shows two tracks to investigate, why they surfaced, and a path into the what-if planner. Track themes below come from [SMU's BSc Information Systems curriculum](https://computing.smu.edu.sg/bsc-information-systems/curriculum), **For Intake AY2025 onwards**, viewed 10 October 2026. The page calls its track course lists *sample* and non-exhaustive; verify requirements and module availability separately by intake.

| Code | Track | Themes evidenced by SMU's sample courses |
|---|---|---|
| BA | Business Analytics | Analytics foundations, big data architecture, AI, machine learning, data mining, social analytics |
| PD | Product Development | Object-oriented programming, cloud engineering, software and systems security, customer relations with analytics |
| FT | Financial Technology | Digital banking architecture, blockchain applications, digital payments, mobile banking |
| SMT | Smart-City Management & Technology | Environmental sustainability, geographic information systems for urban planning, operations research, smart healthcare |

These themes suggest possible study interests; they do not imply that one track exclusively owns a skill, job, or module. The quiz should avoid claiming a track guarantees a career.

## Agreed experience

- 20 questions: five each in Interests, Strengths, Working style, and Career goals.
- Each section alternates question types where possible: three five-point ratings and two single-choice scenarios. Total: 12 ratings and eight scenarios.
- Track labels do not appear in student-facing questions or choices. All scenarios include **E. None of these appeal to me yet**; E and Skip count as unanswered. Students may go back, save, and resume. Require at least three substantive answers in **each** section before calculating results; point out which section needs more answers.
- Ratings have a labelled 1–5 scale. Interests and Career goals: **1 Not interested, 2 Slightly interested, 3 Unsure, 4 Interested, 5 Very interested**. Strengths: **1 Not confident yet, 2 Slightly confident, 3 Unsure, 4 Confident, 5 Very confident**. Working style: **1 Strongly unlike me, 2 Somewhat unlike me, 3 Unsure, 4 Somewhat like me, 5 Very like me**. “Not confident yet” should never be presented as inability or a disqualification.
- Suggested section weights: Interests 30%, Strengths 15%, Working style 20%, Career goals 35%. Results show each track's four section match scores and weighted overall **quiz match score**. They are not statistical percentiles, probabilities of success, or objective measurements until validated against student feedback.
- Show the two highest-ranked tracks even when close or weak. For a close result, say both merit exploration. For a weak result, say the answers did not point strongly to one track. Avoid categorical claims such as “you should choose X.”

## Results content and planner handoff

For each of the top two tracks, show: track name; overall quiz match score; four section scores with their weights; two or three **specific answered items** that contributed most; one honest overlap or uncertainty; and links to (1) the official curriculum and (2) a what-if plan seeded with the track for the student's intake. If the student has sufficient remaining CUs and terms, offer a two-track comparison as a **planner calculation**, not a quiz claim. Keep module recommendations separate from track matching: source modules from the verified intake catalogue, then check prerequisites and term availability before displaying them.

Use short explanation templates only when the cited answers actually exist:

| Track | Possible explanation tied to answers | Exploration prompt |
|---|---|---|
| BA | “Your interest in finding patterns (I1) and choosing evidence-led work (W1 or C2 A) raised this match.” | “Would you enjoy turning messy information into decisions?” |
| PD | “Your interest in improving a useful digital service (I3) and iterating on feedback (S3 or W4 B) raised this match.” | “Would you enjoy building and refining a tool people use?” |
| FT | “Your interest in digital financial services (C3) and attention to transaction risk (S5 or W3) raised this match.” | “Would you enjoy solving trust and usability problems in financial services?” |
| SMT | “Your interest in technology for urban or public outcomes (I5 or C5) and attention to affected communities (W5 or S2 D) raised this match.” | “Would you enjoy designing for places, services, and communities?” |

For a close result: “These two tracks are close in your answers. Compare their sample courses and try both in the planner.” For a weak result: “Your answers span several interests, so no track stands out strongly yet. Explore the top two and revisit the quiz after learning more.” Do not display an invented precision such as 87.43%; the team should decide rounding after validating the score range.

## Team review checklist

1. Ask senior IS students or TAs whether each question reflects a real distinction, whether any language is unclear, and whether any track is stereotyped or underrepresented. Revise wording before assigning final numeric contributions.
2. Test profiles with clear, mixed, and low-interest answers. Check ties, skipped questions, reversals between attempts, and whether all four tracks can plausibly rank first. Keep earlier saved planner choices intact when the quiz is retaken.
3. Confirm whether two saved quiz attempts is a product limit or only a history limit; tell users before a third retake overwrites an attempt.
4. Verify curriculum and module data by intake. The cited public page is for AY2025 onwards; it does not establish requirements for earlier intakes.
5. Keep a record of human edits and survey feedback. The assignment brief permits AI assistance for concepts/sample data but disallows relying on AI for core business logic and main problem solving; disclose AI-reliant parts as required by the brief.
